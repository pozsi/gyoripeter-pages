Appendix 2.

Our plans for the headcount in 2008 in Budapest

We will start recruiting volunteers for the 2008 count in January, 2008. We will approach organizations who are usually active about homeless issues. We will turn to universities and colleges with a training of social work or related subjects. We would like to get high school students involved (they would be accompanied by an adult during the enumeration). We will advertise volunteering in the press. We will also turn to the media to ask people living in Budapest and its surroundings to make a phone call and report about rough sleepers in their neighbourhood (naturally, we will keep this information confidential and offer our help for the rough sleeper in question).

As part of the preparation we shall divide the city into sections that can be covered within one night on foot. The city centre and underpasses are especially important: they should be completely covered. Less central parts of the city shall be divided among the enumerators proportionally.

Each pair of enumerator will receive a section of a map. These maps shall be marked with dots based on the locations known by street workers and/or reported by phone calls, so where we think there are rough sleepers. We will mark those reported by outreach workers with a different colour than those who live in inaccessible (for example private) places.

We will buy as many pens, flashlights, hard surfaces and we can afford to, and will duplicate the registration sheets.

Registration will start on 2008. February 2nd (Saturday), 6 PM and finish on February 3rd, 6 PM. The volunteers will be asked to return the registration sheets on Sunday. Enumerators will have to follow a route marked on their map. They can leave the route, but they should not leave their territory. On the roads that form the boundary of the territory enumerators should only cover their side of the road. The time and place (address) of registration has to be marked on the registration sheet, as well as any landmarks (if needed).

Volunteers have a limited choice of which part of the city they want to cover. The city centre and central, bordering points should have a priority. If we think there will not be enough volunteers to cover these places we will try to involve homeless people living in hostels in the process – we will offer to pay them for their work.

There will be three options of registration on the sheet:

  1. In case of a sleeping homeless person, seen in his “home” the enumerator will have to put down the time, address, sex of the observed and estimated age.
  2. If the enumerator sees someone whom (s)he considers a rough sleeper, but who is not at “home”, (s)he should ask: “Are you homeless?” And “Where will you spend tonight?”. If (s)he says a location in the same section of the city, where the enumerator had already been to or cannot possibly reach, this should be registered. If the person plans to sleep in another section of the city, he will not be registered here. The person shall not be registered if (s)he does not plan to sleep in the same section, even though he usually does. The person should be registered if (s)he does not sleep in any given place at night (but sits on public transport all night, for example) but sits down to rest in the given section. Those people who spend their night wondering around the city, crossing the imaginatory border between sections, should not be registered, even if they spend most of their time in the given section.
  3. We will ask enumerators to mark empty locations if it had been marked on their map or if it seems to be in use, only no one is at “home” in the given time. As volunteers are not professionals, they will need to be instructed about how to decide if a location is in use or not. If we see an empty bed we should mark it as unknown (but put down the gender and age), even if a fellow homeless person can provide information about the missing person. If there are several persons missing, they should all be marked as unknown.

Enumerators should mark their route on the map and at the end of the count hand over the map with the registration sheets. The phone numbers and names of the enumerators should be noted down when they are assigned a territory.

Enumerators receive a sheet of authorization before they go into their territory, which has the details of the headcount, including the name and phone number of the organizers. Upon their return they receive a certificate, and if our finances allow us we will have a party.

We will ask enumerators to only go to locations where they feel safe. If they feel it is not safe they should let us know by telephone and leave that place. They should only register people who do not oppose it. In this situation they should fill out a registration sheet after leaving the person: leave the address empty, but put down the gender and age of the person, as well as indicate that (s)he requested not to be registered.

During the headcount three staff will be constantly available on telephone, and can give advise about any problem arising. There will also be a backup team with three staff, ready to rescue enumerators with their car. The rescue team can also help if there is a rough sleeper who needs or requests specialized help.

On Sunday, in the shelters and hostels we will carry out our usual survey of February 3rd, which contains a maximum of 30 questions (fitted onto one sheet, two pages). The surveys are completed by social workers working in hostels and in shelters. The survey always gives an ID number to the respondent: we use the name which the person tells us. We use the initials of the first two names (Mick Jagger = mj; B. B. King =bb) and their birth date. We use Microsoft Excel which assigns ID numbers to each respondent automatically. We also have the possibility to link the ID number with the gender, in order to eliminate duplication: men generally receive a 1, women a 2. If somebody only has one name, that will be used for his ID number. We need all three data to create the ID number. We ask these questions from everyone in the hostel/shelter who does not object, who arrives in the service before it is too late and who does not fall asleep before the social worker can get there. For those who are sleeping there is a possibility to ask the questions in the morning of the 4th.

It is important not to have too many questions, and to ask questions that can be answered easily and fast. Social workers asking the questions attend an orientation session before February 3rd, where we explain to them the essentials of the research and go through every question. Social workers receive a small sum of money per survey (0.002% of their monthly salary), but we think the service itself should be motivated in taking part, as they receive the data for their service afterwards (not the personal data, only the agglomerated statistics). One survey generally takes 10-15 minutes, one social worker can fill out a maximum of 30 per night.

We make sure that the survey is worded in a way that it can be asked from rough sleepers as well. Rough sleepers are approached by outreach teams, only we leave more time for these surveys to be completed. There is an additional 5 days to cover those rough sleepers whom they have known prior to the headcount and who were registered that night.

Some questions that are used each year for the survey:

  • Are you homeless? Since when? (date)
  • What caused you to become homeless?
  • Who do you live with?
  • Do you have a regular income? Where does it come from?
  • How much money did you spend today?
  • How much money did you have in January?
  • Do you consider yourself healthy enough to work?

We distribute the surveys on February 2nd, they have to be filled out on February 3rd and submitted in the morning of the 4th. We do not hand them over any earlier nor receive them later (with the exception of the outreach teams, where the deadline in February 7th).

We look through all surveys when they are handed back and only pay for them if they are legible and well done.

February 4th we will do a next day survey in day centres. Everyone will be asked where they have spent the previous two nights (the night of the 2nd and the 3rd). We will ask their name and birth date (to form an ID number). If they stayed at a hostel or a shelter, but were not asked any questions, we will ask them now.

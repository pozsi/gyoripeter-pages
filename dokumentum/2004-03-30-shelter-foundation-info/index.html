2004-03-30 Shelter Foundation Info

Shelter Foundation

(Budapest, Hungary)

After the collapse of the communist regime the Shelter Foundation was established among the first non-profit organizations working with homeless people in Hungary. The Foundation has been providing the most important elements of homeless care including non-shelter programs for people living without a home in Budapest. The Foundation has also been organizing the special winter programs of the city of Budapest.

”The aim of the Foundation is to provide shelter for the people living in the capital who because of mental health problems or social circumstances have no shelter or home of their own. By extending its services to the people of Budapest, the Shelter Foundation takes on the broad and complex tasks associated with the social support and reintegration of the homeless.”  

(Extract from the Deed of Foundation).

In November 1989, the homeless people of Budapest staged a mass demonstration at the city’s Southern Railway Station and in the underpass of Blaha Lujza Square. At the time, the homeless had to hide from law enforcement agencies as the contemporary social policies were making the lives of the homeless even harder. But pressure from advocates to change the situation forced the police to modify their tactics. Yielding to the pressure, the Metropolitan Council of the time undertook negotiations with social organizations known for their previous strong interest in social outcasts and the complex issues associated with them. The Shelter Foundation was established in December of that year as a result of these negotiations. The Foundation is liable to receive assistance from any private or legal persons wishing to support its activities.

Operation of Shelter Foundation:

Our basic units are able to carry out their work free of major obstacles. While we are proud of our success, we must deal with the ever-increasing number of homeless people and their deteriorating economic situation and social conditions. Our future goal is to bring recognition and publicity and as a result focused attention to the various units of the Shelter Foundation. The more focused attention can be brought to the problems of the homeless the more help we will get in overcoming the limits and constraints currently preventing a permanent solution.

a/ Homeless Care Center

size of staff: 6 persons

number of people using the service: 20-60

Currently, there are three different situations requiring assistance in this field.

The least complicated is occasional or case-to-case help. In this case a social worker takes part in resolving concrete and distinct problems in the client’s life. These problems such as the acquisition of a rented lodging and the payment of the first month’s rent are relatively easy to handle. Nevertheless, these cases frequently bring about an essential and positive change in the client’s life.

The activity we call “case treatment” is more intense and often of an on-going nature aimed at solving multiple problems. Examples are clients who have some form of income, want to find work or in a situation to claim the social benefits to which homeless people are entitled. These situations usually take longer to resolve and require cooperation from all sides. In these cases our social workers might also get involved in dealing with clients who have psychological and practical problems extending to every aspect of their life. Our goal in these cases is to facilitate a long-term solution and enable the client to manage his/her own life.

Our most recent concern has been the appearance of families with children among the homeless population. The large number of these families with children has not been anticipated. Our goal is to provide safe and secure accommodation for them using available facilities in workers’ hostels or sub-tenancy schemes. In our future work we are planning to create a material base capable of providing existential security for such families.

b/ Information Office

size of staff: 9 social workers

number of people using the service: 80-140/day

The basic task of this unit is to provide proper information, documents and certificates for homeless people. Support for medical treatment and glasses are also available, and it is possible to get photos taken for travel passes and other documents.

We also have a Luggage Depot at the Office. Its function is to allow homeless people to safely deposit their most precious objects and belongings.

c/ Social Services Office

size of staff: 8 social workers

number of people using the service: 60-100/day

The complicated cases that require more time and attention and can not be handled (for technical reasons) at our Information Office are handled here. The primary task of this office is related to the acquisition of different entitlements, e.g. matters associated with unemployment benefits, disablement pensions and regular social allowances are handled here. Should it be necessary we also initiate placements in social homes. If administration outside of Budapest is necessary, we issue ticket vouchers for railway travel, which allows our clients to obtain free railway travel.

We are in contact with offices in charge of mediating accommodations, and we fund access to these for our clients.

Our private attorney provides legal advice once a week and if necessary he acts as representative for our clients in court.

d/ Night Shelter and Home “Vajda 3”

size of staff: 10 social workers

number of people using the service: 50/day

Since 1997 elderly homeless people have been accommodated on the upper floor of the social home. The average age of those living here is 60 and full-board (breakfast, lunch and dinner) is provided for everybody living on this floor. Currently 24 people are living here.

Since 1994, the lower level of the “Vajda 3” home has been operating as a single-night shelter. At present there are 50 beds available every night. In the winter one third of the beds are reserved for the homeless people provided for by the Crisis car service (see below).

The services we provide for homeless people (at both levels) of “Vajda 3” are the following among others: medical care and check-up once a week, hygienic appliances, bed linen, possibility to wash clothes, bathrooms and showers, towels, access to items of clothing and a postal address (at another unit of the Foundation).

e/ The Shelter Hot Line / 24-hour Phone Service

size of staff: 8 social workers

number of calls received: 50-300/day

Since December 1993, this service has been operating with support from the Ministry of Social Welfare. Information regarding the availability of homeless accommodation places in Budapest is available round-the-clock. For the first-stage accommodation units we also provide help by transporting homeless people to the available places. We have also been helping these units by supplying medication and foodstuffs, and mediating other donations. In case a person presumed to be homeless is being looked for we have a list for leaving messages and announcements (the so-called “Missing-list”).

f/ Daytime Program Centers for the Homeless

size of staff: 9 social workers

number of people using the service: 100-120/day

The first of these was opened in January 1994. It is equipped to offer the possibility to wash clothes and take a shower; modest meals (tea and bread) are also provided for the homeless on weekdays between 9 am and 3 pm. In this facility our social workers also help the homeless look for work and housing. The Homeless Job Center and the so-called “Europe Room” also operate here. This facility can cater for up to 120 people at any one time.

In our other Daytime Center special programs are available apart from the general services. In this “Open House Service” programs such as the weekly Movie-Club, and possibilities for entertainment and contact-building such as a small library, card-games, newspapers and magazines, crossword puzzles, indoor games, etc. are also offered.

g/ Crisis car (mobile crisis service)

size of staff: 6

number of places visited: 20-50/week

Our Foundation provides this service for the homeless people who live on the streets of Budapest and may very suddenly get into critical situations (e.g. danger of freezing to death).

This service is in operation night and day from November until April, and twice a week during the summer months. Each time two social workers are on duty with the help of the Foundation’s dispatcher service (24-hour Phone Hot Line). The crisis car visits “popular places” where homeless people spend their time (mostly during the nights). Calls to the Hot Line come from homeless people, street social workers, civilians as well as police officers.

h/ Regional Research and Educational Department

size of staff: 4

The activities of this unit are the following:

- Educational programs for students of social work such as training courses in supervision and opportunities of fieldwork practice;

- Organization of meetings among the managers and heads of shelters and street workers in Budapest and all over the country;

- Research projects:

  • longitudinal surveys (annually on February 3) of homeless people living in shelters and staying in single-night shelters (see: http://www.menhely.hu/publications/after_ten_years_b.htm);
  • Research on hypothermia-related deaths (see: http://www.menhely.hu/publications/hypothermia.pdf).

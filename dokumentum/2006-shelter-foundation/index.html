2006 Shelter Foundation

Shelter Foundation

"It is useless for the social policy establishment of a state to be otherwise perfect; if the satisfactory solution of the housing problem is not secured, the result will remain rather doubtful”

(Dr. HILSCHER Dezső: Introduction to Social Policy)

Our principles, values – Act for the people! Homelessly...

Our principle can be exactly defined as it is summarized in the title of this section. We do anything we can for homeless people – mainly for those who live in Budapest.

Therefore, we understand the needs suffered by people as a result of their homelessness. We work out alternatives to find solutions, and help them to achieve these solutions.

Our principle is to respect life and provide professional assistance. Our objective is to make these adults to be able to behave as adults and to make them able to create conditions for themselves, which gives them dignity.

Our goals, the future

Our Foundation is determined to feel responsibility for handicapped people; our mission is to look after the mental and social needs of homeless in the capital and to re-socialize them. Our activities, therefore, reflect the complexity of the problems and the needs of the homeless. The common goal of our Institute is to fundamentally change the lives of the homeless people who turn to us, as well as to ease their insecurity, bondage and possibly terminate their homelessness.

We also aim to improve our work according to the demands and needs. Apart from just ‘fire-fighting’ we have broadened our services and encourage cooperation and team-working with organizations devoted to look after, and cater to the homeless. Given our extensive experience, we have the background to make decisions on complex issues and problems. We are seeking the solution for the problem of homelessness, making proposals in different fields of social regulation, making proposals for law modifications and we take part in the discussions of these proposals.

Homelessness in Hungary at the turn of millenary and establishment of the Shelter Foundation

In Hungary the situation of the people unable to dissolve the housing of their own has become problematic as of the second half of the nineteenth century. It was tried to treat this multi-handicapped situation in some manner, the system of supply was first built up at the turn of the century and continued to exist just until the end of World War II.

According to the principle of the equality of everybody the homeless life could not be a problem after 1945. Seemingly, even the principle of general employment served to solve the problem of homelessness. This made, in principle, the undertaking of work, in employment relation or in a job category equivalent thereto, an “opportunity” compulsory for everybody. In consequence of the difficulties of employment, of forced industrialisation lots of people lived as commuters, which involved permanent absence from their families, the termination of the earlier network of contacts. The majority of commuters lived in workers' hostels, nurses' hostels, cheap sub-tenancies or night-lodgings.

The most characteristic forms of the denial of homelessness appeared in the authorities’ measures. The people having no housing and work were qualified as vagabonds of public danger, they were handled as trespassers and they could be punished even with imprisonment. Another method of solving the problem was expelling from the larger cities, which urged the people living homeless to roaming.

The deterioration of the situation of world economy affected Hungarian economy too in the 70s. Here we would stress only a detail thereof. The Fund Supporting the Poor (SZETA) was established under the leadership of SOLT Ottília for supporting the groups, which became the poorest in consequence of the changes. SZETA urged, besides providing assistance, the recognition of poverty by the state and the development of organised supply. It was an important step in the development of the systems of supply that, in reply to the increasing need, the first family aid services came into being in 1985-86.

In December 1989 the then-time management of the Hungarian State Railways decided to put a stop to a usage, spreading more and more in the railway stations, that the persons finding no shelter spent the night there more and more regularly. The sitting strike of the homeless held in the centre of the Capital City, in the underpass at Blaha Lujza Square was one of the spontaneous reactions given to the closure at night of the railway stations. The democratic opposition joined the wave of demonstrations, and the progress of events was also monitored by the whole society. Under the influence thereof the state handed over two barracks of the workers’ militia for constructing a night hostel. The hostel, developed in one of the barracks, was operated by the Shelter Foundation, which had been established in consequence of the demonstrations.

The founders of the Foundation were the above-mentioned SZETA, the Social Committee for the Homeless, the Újpest Family Aid Centre, the Patronage Charitable Association and the Metropolitan Council. In our Deed of Foundation we set, as our goal, not only the operation of a night hostel, but the complex assistance to be provided, in a much broader sense of the word, for people having got into a homeless situation.

The Shelter Foundation has continued to develop since that, we have opened newer and newer institutions, starting our units was a reply to the needs discovered in the course of the times.

In the past years the Hungarian legal system has been transformed, Act III of 1993, as amended several times, regulates the social provisions. According to its regulation the state ensures social provision through the local governments. From among the services belonging to the scope of basic social provision (catering, home assistance, family assistance, special tasks of basic provision) the regulated ones must be ensured in each locality. The specialised provision belonging to the scope of personal care shall be offered by the institutions providing nursing and care, the rehabilitation institution, the dwelling home, the institution providing transitory accommodation, the institution providing daytime care and other special social institutions. Among others the home for homeless, the rehabilitation institution and the daytime centre belong to the specialised provisions.

The local government of the locality, the local governments of the metropolitan districts in the Capital City shall ensure the basic provision in the framework of personal care, and it is also a task of the local governments to secure access to the specialised provision for the persons in need, who cannot be cared for in the framework of a basic provision.

Unless the local government of the Capital City and that of a district otherwise agree, in the Capital City the metropolitan local government shall provide for organising and maintaining the night asylum and transitory hostel of the homeless.

Problems of the current regulation

A normative support secured by the state may be claimed for the operational costs of the specialised institutions (night asylum, rehabilitation home of the homeless, transitory hostels, home of the homeless, daytime centre). The rate thereof is changing from year to year, but it is not calculated on the basis of the costs, and it does not cover, in a declared manner, the costs of ensuring the standards prescribed by the professional legal rules.

The Social Act treats the homelessness as an independent problem. According thereto it indicates the place of the people loosing their flats, living homeless within the system of institutions and provisions of the care for homeless, and it does not provide for any progress, to put it more correctly, for the return. The institutions dealing with provision may grant a support for this real alternative, if they win tenders, but these mean an incalculable resource from the aspect of the operation of these organisations.

Besides the organs of the state and the local governments several bodies of interest representation also participate in the constructive promotion of the problem of homelessness. At the turn of 1996 and 1997 a so-called Council of the Ten has been constituted from 10 delegates of the provisioning bodies of the Capital City, where each metropolitan organ assisting the homeless may nominate a member.

The Council holds its meetings monthly, it makes decisions, conceives recommendations and pursues a correspondence with the organs and officials of the Government and ministries, etc. and other organisations. In order to be treated as a partner by the bodies of state administration, the Budapest Association Assisting the Homeless being already a legal entity has also been founded.

Another professional organ of interest representation currently operating is the Metropolitan Council of Homelessness. Its members represent the problem of homelessness from several sides, from the professional part the Council of the Ten participates in the meetings; from the side of the local government these are the representatives of certain departments (of Social Policy, Housing etc.) of the Metropolitan Mayor’s Office and here we find the permanent invitees (from the side of the ministries and authorities). One of the achievements of the Council of Homelessness was, for example, that it has elaborated, on the basis of the legislatory regulation, the concept of a complementary normative support of 30% due to each organisation providing accommodation in the Capital City.

The institutions of the Shelter Foundation are operated by three units, which are separated both organisationally and in respect of economic management. Currently, the following institutions belong to the individual units:

Client Service Office and Daytime Centre

Our Client Service Office and Daytime Centre in Kürt Street are the most frequented institutions of the Capital City providing daytime provision. Our fundamental aim is to provide services, which are open and accessible for everybody, for the mass of the persons in need; to be able to upkeep the professional standard of our work and to provide personalised assistance tailored to individual life situations for the homeless people addressing us.

This Daytime Centre, attached to our Client Service Office, has been operating on weekdays since November 1997. 

The office contributes to the improvement of the income and social situation and health status of the homeless people, it promotes the social integration through mediating basic information, by replacing and obtaining documents and ensuring other services. Besides such servicing and administration activity the editorial office of the newspaper “Roofless” has been operating in our institution since 1997. This newspaper is unique in Europe from the aspect that both the authors (literature and fine arts) and the members of the editorial committee, as well as the distributors of the newspaper are homeless people.

We established our movie club jointly with the people living roofless similarly in 1997. The technical conditions are available (video player, projector), we borrow the movie films from a videotheque, which is well provided and has a continuously expanding supply. The Movie Club functions each Thursday from 2.00 p.m. to 5.00 p.m., under the leadership of two social workers. The number of participants moves between 20 and 50 persons per occasion. The Open House and Crossword Club has been operating similarly since 1997, on Tuesdays. Among our services all the means are to be found which may assist them in the active rest, thus the most popular parlour games, cards, a dozen and a half tablet games, domino, etc. We offer dailies, weeklies and monthly periodicals for reading and we cause the persons puzzling crosswords to tender for the cash prizes on a regular basis. A part of our guests shows himself already as a gaming partner, the contact is palpably different from that in the client service office.

Social Services Office

The activity of our Social Services Office helps enforcing the rights of the persons in need in a manner unique in the Capital City and even at the national level, it facilitates their getting employed through its benefits (obtaining an official certificate of clean criminal record, replacement of driving licence, etc.), it promotes the normalisation of their contact with their families (voucher for travel). According to our experiences the operation of the organs of the state and the local governments shows serious deficiencies, primarily in the field of information. They do not stress sufficiently the provision of their clients with accurate information worded in an understandable manner. It is also characteristic for the functioning of the offices that each institution can only provide information about the service offered by it.

In addition to providing assistance in the matters of individuals, one of our most important aims is to try to solve the typical problems relating to competence in general, over and above the individual cases, at the universal level. Thus our clients could in several cases address, with our help, the competent court, the administration office and, in more than one case, the Office of Parliament Commissioners  [Ombudsman].

Our general services and benefits are the following in this division:

Full-range administration of pensions, assistance in receiving unemployment, maternity, local government provisions, handicapped benefit, arrangement of complex matters like sick-pay, labour law issues, tax returns, applications for compensation, etc. Our legal aid service gives assistance primarily in putting down statements of claim and pleadings, and this is complemented by giving oral advice. The matters of divorce, the issues of estate and inheritance, the transgressions and the civil litigious proceedings, where most often the re-acquisition of the flats acquired through fraud from the client earlier by the way of fictitious flat exchanges is at stake, continue to be the typical matters. We give credited travel vouchers for the administration of matters in the countryside, and, once a year, to anybody for visiting the family, and to everybody in the three days preceding Christmas and Easter.

Care Centre

The social workers of the Shelter Foundation have been dealing with the caring for the homeless since 1989.

Apart from the concrete provision we consider the assistance realised through personal contacts to be efficient. In the Care Centre 14 social workers with college or university degree are working. The average of their working time spent with our foundation is 102 months. Our collaborators work in accordance with a pre-determined order of duty in the client service offices and the Care Centre, and they carry out the individual and family care for the homeless in flexible working time. It is a part of their work to receive their clients, at a time agreed on jointly, in the office in Baross Street and to accompany them to various offices, authorities or to proceed in their stead and name in different matters on the basis of a power of attorney-in-fact. They visit their clients in the hostels, on the street and in health care institutions on a regular basis. It is a part of our work to help the clients understanding the information concerning them, relating to their lives and health status, to provide assistance in the processing thereof and to mediate between the client and the other institutions.

Our activity relating to the individual and family care for the homeless has been made more difficult, for several years, by the deepening of the prejudices relating to the homeless and homelessness. This is largely deteriorating the chances of our clients for arranging their situation, since their homelessness has become a stigma, a shameful secret determining each field of their lives. Thereby also the work of the social workers dealing with them became more difficult, since, in the process of care, it became a particular task to clear up the individual’s relation to his own situation and the prejudices inflicting him, without which the failure of the process of care can be encoded in advance. We must become aware, in the first phase of the care, of the image the homeless people have developed about themselves, we must know, which were the

 atrocities they had been inflicted with, which were the losses they had been faced to and we must help processing all this at their own pace and taking utmost into consideration their capacity.

Dispatcher Service

The collaborators of our Dispatcher Service are waiting, 24 hours a day, the telephone calls of the people living roofless, of the social workers working in their interest, of the employees of health care and other institutions and of the population, in connection with homeless people having got into a critical situation, at the Budapest telephone number 338-4186. In order to publicise our service we appear in paid and free advertisements in the streets, institutions and the mass media on a continuous basis. 

The system of institutions for caring for the homeless has been built up at nationwide and metropolitan levels in the past twelve years. In Budapest, more than 150 institutions are dealing with the provision of the homeless, and besides them there are numerous other social organisations providing services also for them. It follows from the high number of the institutions and the provided people that the exchange of information, the continuous communication and the easily accessible and accurate information supply for the persons in need and for the social workers working in their interest are absolutely necessary for the efficient operation of the services. Therefore we set up our telephone service on 15 November 1994. In the past years we contacted each metropolitan institution known to us, we collect the data of more than 250 institutions. If required, not only the Budapest, but also nationwide information is at our disposal. 

Due, on the one hand, to the complexity of the institutional system providing for the homeless, and, on the other hand, to the high number of the homeless living here, it is almost impossible to find individuals in the Capital City, although according to our experiences it is rather frequent that institutions, social workers, family members and friends are looking for the people, about whom it can be supposed that they are in contact with some of the organisations. For solving this problem we have elaborated a system, through which we try to achieve that the “disappeared” persons should know that somebody is looking for them. The essence thereof is

that we mediate messages to the homeless, in which we tell, by whom and in what a manner they are searched for.

Our service is also participating in organising professional trainings for the collaborators of night asylums, street care services, and for those of the social institutions of Pest County.

Crisis Car

We operated the Crisis Car in winter 1998/1999 for the first time, after having processed the experiences of our Tea Line established for the first time in the Capital City in winter 1993 and the experiences of our Dispatcher Service operating since December 1994, relating to the homeless people living on the street.

It is the purpose of operating the Crisis Car that trained and experienced social workers can visit, within a relatively short time, the homeless people getting into a crisis situation, into circumstances jeopardising their lives and limbs and mental health, in any part of Budapest, 24 hours a day during the winter season, who can help in the solution of such a dangerous situation.

In order to obtain knowledge of the homeless people having got into a difficult situation we try to draw the population’s attention to our services, we request their assistance in newspaper articles and TV programmes and we co-operate with the Budapest Headquarters of Police and the National Ambulance Service. We await the reports at the telephone number 338-4186 of the Dispatcher Service operating 24 hours a day, our person on duty will consider, on the basis of the information received, what he will do, whether he calls the ambulance, provides information or sends a social worker to the site.

The health status of the people living on the street is tragic; due to their disorderly nutrition and weakened organism they are exposed to an increased danger of freezing, and due to their way of life their other sicknesses become more and more serious and incurable; according to our experiences many of them cannot survive the months of winter without help.

The activities of the Crisis Car operating 24 hours a day are implemented at a scale, which begins with offering food, clothing and medicines in the nature of first aid, lasts from the provision of information relating to the system of social and health care institutions, through the transportation to the hostels, and through mediating to other social and health care institutions up to the permanent maintenance of contacts and of a caring relationship.

„Vajda 3” Asylum

Our asylum in Vajdahunyad Street started its operations as one of the first institutions of the system of provision dealing with homeless people, reorganised as of the democratic transformation in winter 1989. The activity of our unit has undergone considerable changes in comparison with the beginning, we have been renewing and qualitatively improving our institution since October 1996 on a continuous basis.

  • Social Home of the Homeless: “Upper Level”

We have established our upper level to care for old-aged, ill men with deteriorated health status, who are unable to maintain themselves and would be exposed, in the narrow sense of the word, to life-danger on the street. According to the experiences the average age of our patients is around 65 years. The people dwelling here have no family and other external contacts, due to their age and health status the transitory accommodation of them does not mean a solution.

In our institution we provide the following services: accommodation: in rooms with 3 to 5 beds; regular medical attendance; dosing of medicines, provision of medicines; everyday supervision by nurse; hairdresser, pedicurist, manicurist, physiotherapist; three meals a day; unlimited use of our stock of clothes; TV, video; organised events. We consider the development of our in-house health care provision to be of a stable and reliable standard and the introduction of regular physiotherapy and massage to be significant results.

  • Night Asylum

Our night hostel is open from 6.00 p.m. till 8.00 a.m. Our services: bread and drippings, tea, towel and soap, possibility for bathing and washing, washing and drying the clothes, watching

the TV, cooking and warming the meals; health care provision in justified cases, basic medicines, giving information and advice. 

The conditions of access to the asylum are simple: if we have free places, they may be used by any adult man of Hungarian citizenship, if he has a certificate of pulmonary screening not older than 1 year and a certificate on disinfection issued by the State People’s Health Care and Medical Officer’s Service not older than one week (we do not insist on the certificates for the first occasion).

  • Room for convalescents (crisis division)

We have established this form of provision for the accommodation of and caring for people transported in by the various street care services in the winter period. Our convalescent division provides the following services: survey of the health and social status; dosing of medicines; provision of medicines; doctor’s visit three times a week; bandage and treatment of wounds; replacement of documents, administration (social security card, identity card, home address card, procedure for the entitlement to invalidity pension, acquisition of income, forwarding to other institutions).

The persons having got here have spent 3 to 4 weeks in the average in the crisis division. There are persons who use our services for 1 to 2 days, there are ones who use them for 3 months.

Daytime Centre in Práter Street

The Shelter Foundation has been operating its Daytime Centre in Budapest, District VIII since January 1994, in the cellar to be found in Práter Street 29/b. The daytime centre fills a double function with its services. One of them is to mitigate the difficulties resulting from homeless life, to try to make supportable and liveable the lives of the homeless people. We call our services to be classified in this category “basic services”, and they may be used practically by any person in need. These services of ours serve the needs of persons having no possibility for dwelling; those needs can be satisfied by the civilians forming the majority of society in their

homes. I.e. it is possible to wash, to take a shower, to shave, to wash the clothes, to have meals, to take a rest, to read, to carry out leisure activities (to play cards or chess, to play at merils, to puzzle crosswords, to write a letter) in our daytime centre. Here we classify our services, through which we provide assistance for solving the problems, which result from the special way of life of the people living roofless (arrangement of everyday matters, obtaining of documents or the replacement thereof, searching for accommodation). Our daytime centre has been carrying out social work on the street too, since summer 2000, which also belongs to our basic services.

In our social work on the street we consider important that the people we have got acquainted with, living on the street, should realise: they can improve their situation even by merely trying to use one of the institutions caring for the homeless.

We deal also with the mediation of work, and the provision of support for sub-tenancy is our new programme. This may be used by clients who are already in a contact of care with one of our collaborators and agree to co-operate. We have an own flat, in which a client fit for independent life, who is also able to make regular savings, can move in on the basis of a decision of the social workers. Our aim is to enable him to rent a flat even in market conditions after a transitory period and to lay thereby the foundation of his own life, independent of the assisting institutions.

Methodology

The methodology group of our foundation has been operating as an independent organisational unit since 1998. The law protection forum is the oldest activity of the programme, where we deal with the solution of the injuries in the field of law the homeless people are inflicted by. Our service is more and more in the nature of mediation, which is positioned between the legal aid and the interest representation, and it means an experiment for interest reconciliation carried out with assisting purpose outside the traditional institutions of justice. The methodology is also organising researches related to the homeless. Thus we have carried out a data survey based on questionnaire among the homeless people of the Capital City always on the same day for several years. The aim of the questionnaire is to monitor the changes and we inquire annually about a special scope of questions. Besides that we have surveyed the legislatory background

in connection with the domestic cases of cooling and the administration activity ensuring the registration thereof. In order to prevent the cases of death we made a proposal to the competent ministries for the amendment to the legal rule. Our most recent research examined the providing of aid for the homeless living in Budapest and the differences between the legal prescriptions and the practice. In the course of the research it appeared that the criticism relating to the provision of aid is well-founded, namely the offices did not act according to the rules of the state administration procedure in the matters of the majority of the applicants. As of autumn 2003 our foundation has been a metropolitan Field Institution of Methodology. In the course of our work we surveyed, which were the field opportunities asked by the institutions training social specialists, and we try to harmonise these needs with the institutional possibilities.

In spring 2002 we started a campaign, the primary purpose of which was to diminish the prejudices relating to the homeless people. From the aspect of the required assistance, homelessness is somewhat totally different from what is thought of it by the public opinion, and it differs considerably from what is considered a necessary action by the politics and the social administration. The commonplaces spread in connection with homelessness force the decision-makers to remain permanently within the scope of actions aimed for crisis prevention. We wanted to present the people, who are not considered homeless according to the generally accepted opinions, although they experience the same things, which are experienced by the people called homeless, on the basis of the well-known characteristic features. We presented the blood and flesh homelessness through our messages displayed in pictures. Through shaping the public opinion we wished to break the future way of a model of assistance, which is truly problem focused and concentrates more on the individual. The “sensitising campaign”, as we called it, is a series of posters, the slogan of which is:

“Act for the people! Homelessly...”

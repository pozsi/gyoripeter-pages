Short policy paper on situation in Hungary - Keserű Zsolt

Munkaerő-piaci reintegráció a hajléktalan-ellátásban

Pályázati azonosító: A/57, FT id.: HU–32

EMIR azonosító: EQUAL–2.1.1–2005–10–0008/10

Policy papers and priorities for the social reintegration of the homeless

Sources:

  • Joint Memorandum on Social Inclusion - 2003
  • National Action Plan 2002-2006
  • Government decrees
  • National Report for FEANTSA - 2006

Achievements of policies directly related to services to the homeless

  • Several forms of services to the homeless have developed: providing shelter, daycare, outreach services (working in the streets). Non-governmental organisations have played an important part in setting up and managing services.
  • At the end of the year 2003, approximately 6200 beds were available for the homeless at night shelters and temporary hostels.
  • A ministerial commissioner has been in charge of managing the existing problems and coordinating policies and the activities of the service providers since 2002.
  • Steps have been taken to coordinate social and medical services to the homeless. Non-stop medical centres have been set up, including infirmaries.  
  • 900 people are given subsidisation for housing outside institutionalised care as an important step in reintegration.
  • A Parliament Decree was passed in 2003 on the prevention of family homelessness.
  • Normative subsidisation of outreach services was introduced in 2005.

Aims and priorities in the comprehensive treatment of homelessness

  • Strengthening programs providing ways out of homelessness. Since 2000, special funds have been available for model projects on employing the homeless and assisting independent housing. Nevertheless, prevention and reintegration programs have reached few of those in need.
  • Besides providing care for those already homeless, it is an important task to set up model programs aiming at reviewing and improving the conditions of prevention and reintegration.
  • The is essential to review current policies on the system of care for the homeless and the minimum guarantees of housing.
  • It is essential to improve cooperation between ministries responsible for medical care, employment and housing.
  • The current financing system is not flexible: it encourages the establishment of more beds, not services. The financing system should be modified to reflect the achievements and results of different kinds of services.
  • Beds and housing possibilities outside institutionalised care need to be extended, as they are not only more cost-effective, but also provide steps for independent living if properly managed.
  • Extending social services independent from hostels and shelters.
  • Developing daytime care: more efficient organising, setting up dispatcher (hotline) services for better access to information.

Achievements and priorities of policies indirectly related to homeless services

Housing policy

  • Housing subsidies are sporadic and not systematic in Hungary: support reaches out to 5 to 7% of homes in the form of occasional assistance or donations in kind, and amounts to 0.2-0.3% of the GDP. In the EU, 18% of homes receive housing support, using 1% of the GDP. Housing subsidisation schemes run by local governments often do not reach those who need it most due to administrative omissions, lack of information or discrimination.
  • The Social Act of 2003 assigns the task of handling debts and arrears to the local governments. The service provided rests on two pillars: on one hand, local governments give cash subsidies (90% of which from the central state budget) towards paying off debts; on the other hand, they promote the debtor’s self-sufficiency by giving financial advice.
  • Preventive measures, such as the extension of household maintenance subsidies, are useful in reducing the accumulation of debts related to housing. In the meantime, financial advisers need to be trained.

Employment policy

  • Regarding employment, the biggest challenge is the high ratio of inactive population (in the population aged 15 to 64, it is 40.2% in Hungary and 30% in EU-15). Employment rate is also below the EU average (Hungary: 56%, EU-15: 30%). The employment rate of both males and females is far below the EU average and the targets set out in Lisbon (for the years 2005 and 2010).
  • Within integrated and streamlined approaches and services, training provided for the most disadvantaged strata takes central importance. The number of participants must be increased, and new programs must be started with the aim of increasing the employment skills and possibilities of the disadvantaged.

Health policy

  • Social and health services are essential in reducing inequality and discrimination. Services tailored to the needs of certain groups and exploring the factors that prevent access to basic health care are a priority.
  • It is an important objective to improve the medical condition of disadvantaged groups (Romas, addicts, homeless) and to reach out to those living in slums, streets and shelters by reducing the gap between people and services (e.g. with the help of mobile health screening units).
  • It is an important objective to provide access to prevention campaigns, screenings and therapy for the disadvantaged.
  • It is important to develop the cooperation of health and social services regarding care for mental illnesses.

Social care system

  • The main priority is the development of accessible and humane services responding to real needs, including basic and community services and the restructuring of large institutions. Models need to be set up to provide good examples for the integration of basic social and health care.
  • Due to the increase in the number of alcohol and drug addicts and abusers, community service forms, daytime care, rehabilitation and temporary care institutions need to be extended and developed. It is an important aim to provide more extensive geographical coverage and more differentiated services depending on the clients’ condition.
  • The social reintegration role of social care needs to be strengthened with the help of systematic development of homeless services, special assistance services, and by starting model programs aiming at boosting the reintegration chances of people leaving institutionalised care.
